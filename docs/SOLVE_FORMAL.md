# Route B (formal): does `success` go high, and is the input unique?

Formal search on the **extracted netlist** (`out/solve_b/puzzle.v`, regenerated by
`tools/retrace/extract.py` from `upstream/puzzle.gds`) — not the recovered RTL, and not
constrained by anyone's interpretation of what the design does. The harness
(`formal/solve_top.sv`) only uses the facts the task gives directly: one clock, an
active-low reset on `rst_n`, a free single-bit input `I`, and a hold-line `enable`. It
does not read or depend on `docs/INTENT.md`, `docs/SOLVE_ANALYTICAL.md`,
`tools/solve/starbattle*`, or `out/solve_a/` (route A, running independently).

Reproduce everything below with:

```
.venv/bin/python -m tools.solve.formal_solve
```

## 1-2. Harness and search

`formal/solve_top.sv` instantiates `puzzle` (the extracted netlist) with:

- `rst_n` low only in the first cycle, high forever after;
- `enable` high on every cycle from the second cycle on (no gaps) — the "simplest
  first" policy the task asked to try first;
- `I` completely free every cycle (the solver chooses it);
- `cover (success);` (default) or, with `` `define SOLVE_BMC``, `assert (!success);`.

Cell behaviour comes from the Liberty functions
(`read_liberty -ignore_miss_func sky130_fd_sc_hd__tt_025C_1v80.lib`), tap/decap/fill/
diode cells are deleted, and the design is flattened and prepped exactly as
`formal/warmup_equiv.sby` and `tools/analysis/e2e.py` already do for this repo's other
SymbiYosys proofs.

`formal/solve.sby` runs four SBY tasks in parallel (`sby --prefix out/solve_b/solve -j 4
-f formal/solve.sby cover_yices cover_bitwuzla bmc_abc bmc_yices`), depth 135 (the design
needs ~121 enabled cycles plus the reset cycle and register-propagation margin):

| task | mode | engine | result | depth reached | wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cover_yices` | cover | `smtbmc yices` | **cover(success) reached** | step 123 | 7 s |
| `cover_bitwuzla` | cover | `smtbmc bitwuzla` | **cover(success) reached** | step 123 | 12 s |
| `bmc_abc` | bmc | `abc bmc3` | **assert(!success) failed** (counterexample) | step 123 | 4 s |
| `bmc_yices` | bmc | `smtbmc yices` | **assert(!success) failed** (counterexample) | step 123 | 6 s |

All four tasks — two different formulations (does a trace reaching `success` exist? /
does `success` ever violate "never"?) run through three different solvers (Yices,
Bitwuzla, ABC's own SAT-based `bmc3`) — agree: **yes, `success` is reachable, and every
one of them finds it at exactly the same depth (step 123)**, well inside the 135-cycle
bound. This is real solving, not a bound artifact: see "Pitfalls" below for the check
that ruled out the obvious formal false positive (uninitialised state), and note that
`cover_yices` and `cover_bitwuzla` — independently, different solvers — reported the
identical 121-bit sequence.

## 4. Uniqueness

`formal/solve_uniq.sby` re-runs the same four engine/mode combinations on
`formal/solve_uniq_top.sv`: identical harness, plus a sticky `differs` register that
latches once `I` disagrees with `FOUND_I[]` (the sequence above, `` `include``d from
`out/solve_b/solve_found_bits.vh`) on any of the first 121 enabled cycles, and the
property becomes `cover(success && differs)` / `assert(!(success && differs))`.

| task | mode | engine | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cover_yices` | cover | `smtbmc yices` | **cover(success && differs) NOT reached** (depth 135) |
| `cover_bitwuzla` | cover | `smtbmc bitwuzla` | **cover(success && differs) NOT reached** (depth 135) |
| `bmc_abc` | bmc | `abc bmc3` | **assert(!(success && differs)) held** (depth 135) |
| `bmc_yices` | bmc | `smtbmc yices` | **assert(!(success && differs)) held** (depth 135) |

All four agree: **no other 121-bit sequence reaches `success` within the bound.**

**Exact scope of that claim.** It covers every input where:

- `rst_n` is low for exactly the first cycle and high for every cycle after (same reset
  policy as the search);
- `enable` is high on every cycle from the second cycle through at least cycle 135 —
  the *contiguous, "simplest first"* policy the task named — no gaps, no early or late
  start;
- `I` is unconstrained on each of those cycles;
- the trace is bounded to 135 cycles.

Within that box, BMC to depth 135 is exhaustive: at every step k <= 135 the solver
proves no assignment of the (still fully free) suffix of `I` reaches
`success && differs`, which is exactly "no other input of this shape, of any length up
to 135 cycles, succeeds." Since the design itself decides at cycle 123 in every trial
(both here and in step 1-2, independently, across all four engines), 135 gives 12
cycles of margin past the observed decision point.

**What it does NOT cover** (honestly, per the task's own framing, not explored further
here for lack of time):

- **Enable gaps.** The task asked to try `enable` held high throughout first, "then also
  consider whether enable gaps could matter" — that second half was not run. A sequence
  entered with pauses (enable dropped and re-raised) is a different, larger search
  (variable-length traces, not just variable content) and is not ruled out by this
  result.
- **A different reset policy** (e.g. `rst_n` low for more than one cycle, or asserted
  again mid-run) is not explored; the extracted netlist's own flops reset on any cycle
  `rst_n` is low (used as a plain structural fact, not an interpretation of the puzzle),
  so a longer reset should not change the result, but this was not formally checked.
- **Traces longer than 135 cycles.** This is bounded model checking, not an unbounded
  induction proof (`mode prove`/PDR was not used here) — a hypothetical success path
  needing more than 135 cycles is outside the bound. Given the decision consistently
  lands at cycle 123 across two independent 4-engine runs, this is considered very low
  risk, but it is a real limit of BMC, not a proof.

## Pitfalls hit while building this (reported because they change how much to trust the
above, and because the task says never to paper over what didn't work)

1. **`localparam bit [...]`** (SystemVerilog's `bit` keyword) does not parse under plain
   `read_verilog` (no `-sv`) — fixed by dropping `bit` (`formal/solve_uniq_top.sv`'s
   included header uses plain `localparam [127:0] FOUND_I = ...`).
2. **The first full run of `solve.sby`'s `bmc_abc`/`bmc_yices` tasks failed almost
   instantly** (`Assert failed`, elapsed ~0 s) on an *earlier, unguarded* version of the
   harness. That is the classic formal false positive: at cycle 0, before any clock edge,
   every internal flop's initial state is unconstrained, so a garbage combinational read
   of `success` before reset takes effect can trivially violate `assert(!success)`. This
   was ruled out by construction, not patched over: the properties are asserted from a
   solid, already-reset design state, and the final trace (above) was inspected directly
   and shows `I` toggling through all 121 real cycles with `success` only rising at the
   very end (step 123) — not a step-0 artifact. (See `formal/solve_top.sv`'s header
   comment for the reasoning.)
3. **`abc bmc3` initially errored** on the uniqueness harness only
   (`ERROR: Design contains 'x' or 'z' bits`, from `write_aiger`), while `solve_top.sv`
   (no `differs` logic) was fine. Root cause: `FOUND_I[cyc[6:0]]` is a 7-bit-selected mux
   over a 121-entry table; Yosys synthesizes *all* 128 decode branches regardless of
   whether `cyc[6:0]` can dynamically reach codes 121-127, and a `[120:0]`-only array
   leaves those branches as Verilog's defined-but-unconstrained out-of-range value —
   `x` — baked into the netlist structurally. The SMT2 backend tolerates that; the AIGER
   backend `abc bmc3` needs does not. Fixed by declaring `FOUND_I` as `[127:0]` (121 real
   bits + 7 defined-zero padding bits), eliminating the `x` at its source rather than
   gating around it.
4. **The first corrected uniqueness run reported "another solution exists"** — every
   engine flipped (`cover` PASS, `bmc` FAIL) — which turned out to be a bug in
   `tools/solve/formal_solve.py`'s bit-packing, not a real second solution: the 7 padding
   bits were concatenated on the wrong (low) end of the 128-bit literal, silently
   shifting the real 121 bits up by 7 positions, so the harness was comparing `I` against
   the *wrong* found-bit at every cycle. Caught by decoding the "differing" counterexample
   trace and finding it bit-for-bit *identical* to the found sequence (0 of 121 bits
   different) despite `differs` reading 1 — i.e. the comparator itself was broken, not
   the design. Fixed by reordering the concatenation (`"0"*7 + bits[::-1]`, verified
   against a direct Python re-implementation of the bit-select before rerunning SBY) and
   confirmed independently with a throwaway `cover(differs)`-only harness (ignoring
   `success` entirely) that the `differs` mechanism does trigger, quickly, on a
   deliberately different `I` — i.e. it is not vacuously stuck at 0. After the fix, all
   four engines agree (table above), and re-running the whole pipeline via
   `tools.solve.formal_solve` from scratch reproduces the identical result.

## Files

- `formal/solve_uniq_top.sv`, `formal/solve_uniq.sby` — step 4 (uniqueness).

  tools.solve.formal_solve` regenerates everything in this document from scratch.

  sequence.

  dirs for each phase (VCDs, SMT2, logs).

## Scope closed (lead review, 2026-09-19)

The enable-gap and depth caveats above are closed by `test/test_uniqueness.py`: a SAT proof on
the extracted netlist that `enable` = 0 before the decision changes no state, plus the latched
decision after it. See `docs/SOLUTION.md`. The solution is unique over all input sequences.
